Every Score Is a Crowd

27,075 games. One number each. We went looking for good games and kept finding the people who rated them.

Act 1 — The fog

A bigger crowd does not hand out better scores

Every dot is a game: how many people reviewed it, and how many of them were positive. Click a band — the five columns behind the bars — and watch which end of the cloud moves.

Act 2 — The rulers

Every kind of game is judged against its own crowd

Nineteen kinds of games, one row each. Switch the ruler to see how far apart they really are, then pick a crowd size and watch the disagreement inside each kind shrink — the order never moves.

Ruler

Act 3 — The cut

The same chart, cut three ways

One line, three cuts. The score rises to a plateau somewhere between 8 and 32 hours, then sags back — so the question is whether longer games are better, or whether the boxes change who is standing in them. Cutting by kind of game only splits the level, never the shape: its groups are crowds in disguise. Cutting by crowd shifts every point up or down by about five, and the arch is still there inside each line.

Eight playtime bins. The grey bars are how many games sit in each one; the coloured strip is who those games are, and it moves with the cut.

Act 4 — Your turn

Every kind of game, at every size of crowd

A toolbox, not a verdict: nineteen kinds down, six crowds across. Each cell holds the middle 80% of that cell's scores — the thin line runs from the 10th to the 90th percentile, the thick bar is the middle half, the dot is the median. Every cell is shaded by its own median, so the darker half of the grid is the half that scores high.

Click a cell — or move through them with the arrow keys — to see its numbers and three real games drawn from it.

Data: Steam Store Games (Kaggle, nikdavis), a SteamSpy snapshot from September 2019. SteamSpy is an unofficial project and is not associated with Valve.
